## 2. Overview

Project management is commonly organized around five process groups: Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, and Closing.

Depending on the organization, industry, project methodology, and delivery approach, additional stages or workstreams may be managed separately. These activities provide more detailed control over feasibility, requirements, design, procurement, testing, deployment, operational transition, and benefits realization.

These stages complement the five process groups rather than universally replacing them. Monitoring and controlling occurs throughout the project lifecycle, while Agile projects may repeat planning, development, testing, and delivery activities in iterative cycles.

## 3. Additional Project Management Stages and Activities

| Stage or Workstream | Purpose | Key Activities | Typical Deliverables |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feasibility and Business Analysis | Determine whether the project is viable and aligned with business objectives. | Assess business needs, costs, benefits, constraints, alternatives, risks, and organizational readiness. | Feasibility study, business case, cost-benefit analysis, recommendation. |
| Requirements Gathering | Document business, user, functional, nonfunctional, and technical needs. | Conduct interviews, workshops, surveys, stakeholder analysis, and requirements prioritization. | Requirements document, user stories, requirements traceability matrix, acceptance criteria. |
| Design and Solution Development | Define the proposed solution and how it will meet approved requirements. | Develop solution architecture, process designs, prototypes, workflows, and technical specifications. | Solution design, architecture diagrams, prototypes, design specifications. |
| Procurement and Contracting | Acquire the products, services, equipment, and vendor support needed for delivery. | Define procurement requirements, evaluate suppliers, review proposals, negotiate contracts, and manage vendors. | Procurement plan, request for proposal, vendor evaluation, contract, purchase order. |
| Testing and Validation | Confirm that deliverables meet requirements and work as intended. | Develop test plans, conduct functional and integration testing, validate user acceptance, log defects, and retest fixes. | Test plan, test cases, defect log, test results, user acceptance sign-off. |
| Implementation and Deployment | Put the approved solution, service, or program into operation. | Prepare the deployment, coordinate resources, train users, execute rollout, communicate changes, and monitor initial performance. | Deployment plan, implementation checklist, training materials, release record, go-live approval. |
| Transition and Handover | Transfer ownership and operational responsibility to the receiving team. | Prepare support documentation, transfer knowledge, confirm access, train operational staff, and agree on support procedures. | Handover checklist, operating procedures, support plan, training records, handover acceptance. |
| Benefits Realization | Measure whether the expected business and program benefits are achieved. | Establish baseline measures, monitor outcomes, compare actual results with targets, and report variances. | Benefits realization plan, KPI dashboard, outcome reports, benefits review. |
| Post-Implementation Review | Evaluate project performance and identify lessons and improvements. | Review delivery outcomes, budget, schedule, quality, stakeholder satisfaction, risks, and lessons learned. | Post-implementation review, lessons-learned register, improvement plan, final recommendations. |

## 4. Detailed Stage Activities

### 4.1 Feasibility and Business Analysis

**Objective:** Establish whether the proposed project is practical, affordable, and aligned with strategic goals.

Activities:
- Define the business problem or opportunity.
- Identify intended beneficiaries and stakeholders.
- Assess technical, operational, financial, and schedule feasibility.
- Evaluate available resources and potential funding constraints.
- Compare alternative solutions.
- Identify major risks, dependencies, and assumptions.
- Recommend whether to proceed, revise, defer, or stop the project.

**Success measure:** An evidence-based recommendation and documented decision to proceed or take another appropriate action.

### 4.2 Requirements Gathering

**Objective:** Establish clear, measurable requirements before solution design and delivery.

Activities:
- Identify stakeholders and their needs.
- Conduct interviews, workshops, surveys, and requirements sessions.
- Document business, functional, technical, security, accessibility, and operational requirements as applicable.
- Prioritize requirements based on value, urgency, risk, and feasibility.
- Define acceptance criteria.
- Validate requirements with authorized stakeholders.
- Maintain traceability from requirements to deliverables and testing.

**Success measure:** Requirements are documented, reviewed, prioritized, and approved by the appropriate stakeholders.

### 4.3 Design and Solution Development

**Objective:** Translate approved requirements into a practical solution.

Activities:
- Develop solution concepts and workflows.
- Evaluate design alternatives.
- Define system architecture or program delivery models.
- Create prototypes, mockups, process maps, or technical specifications where appropriate.
- Review security, privacy, usability, accessibility, and maintainability requirements.
- Obtain design reviews and required approvals.
- Update designs based on documented feedback.

**Success measure:** The proposed design addresses approved requirements and is ready for authorized implementation.

### 4.4 Procurement and Contracting

**Objective:** Obtain the goods and services necessary to deliver the project.

Activities:
- Identify procurement needs and specifications.
- Establish procurement schedules and budget limits.
- Develop vendor selection criteria.
- Issue requests for information or proposals when appropriate.
- Evaluate vendor qualifications, cost, quality, and delivery capability.
- Complete required legal, security, financial, and compliance reviews.
- Execute authorized contracts and monitor vendor performance.

**Success measure:** Required goods and services are acquired through approved procedures and managed against contract requirements.

### 4.5 Testing and Validation

**Objective:** Verify that the project deliverables satisfy documented requirements and acceptance criteria.

Activities:
- Prepare test plans and test scenarios.
- Conduct inspections, functional testing, integration testing, or pilot evaluations as appropriate.
- Record defects, issues, and nonconformities.
- Assign corrective actions and track resolution.
- Conduct regression testing when needed.
- Obtain user acceptance or designated reviewer approval.
- Retain testing evidence and acceptance records.

**Success measure:** Required tests and reviews are completed, critical issues are resolved or formally accepted, and approval evidence is documented.

### 4.6 Implementation and Deployment

**Objective:** Introduce the approved solution or program into its intended operating environment.

Activities:
- Confirm implementation readiness.
- Validate staffing, schedules, resources, and dependencies.
- Prepare deployment and contingency plans.
- Communicate implementation timelines and responsibilities.
- Train users, educators, facilitators, or operational teams as appropriate.
- Execute rollout or program launch activities.
- Monitor early performance and resolve implementation issues.
- Document approval to proceed with the operational launch.

**Success measure:** The solution or program is implemented according to the approved plan and required readiness and acceptance criteria.

### 4.7 Transition and Handover

**Objective:** Ensure that the receiving team can operate and support the delivered solution or program.

Activities:
- Identify the receiving operational owner.
- Transfer documentation, procedures, schedules, and relevant records.
- Complete knowledge-transfer sessions.
- Confirm training, access, support resources, and escalation procedures.
- Document unresolved issues and ongoing responsibilities.
- Confirm maintenance and support arrangements.
- Obtain handover acceptance.

**Success measure:** The receiving team acknowledges ownership and has the documentation, training, and resources needed to assume responsibility.

### 4.8 Benefits Realization

**Objective:** Determine whether the project produces its intended business, educational, or operational outcomes.

Activities:
- Define measurable benefits and baseline performance.
- Assign owners responsible for tracking each benefit.
- Establish measurement methods and reporting intervals.
- Compare actual outcomes with approved targets.
- Evaluate adoption, participation, performance, cost efficiency, and stakeholder feedback as applicable.
- Document benefits not yet achieved and proposed corrective actions.
- Continue tracking benefits after project delivery when necessary.

**Success measure:** Outcomes are measured against defined targets and supported by reliable evidence.

### 4.9 Post-Implementation Review

**Objective:** Assess the project's effectiveness and capture lessons for future initiatives.

Activities:
- Compare actual scope, schedule, and costs with approved baselines.
- Review deliverable quality and acceptance.
- Assess stakeholder satisfaction and operational readiness.
- Evaluate realized benefits and outstanding commitments.
- Review risk responses, change requests, and issue resolution.
- Identify successful practices and improvement opportunities.
- Assign owners and due dates for agreed improvement actions.
- Publish the final review and lessons learned.

**Success measure:** A documented review identifies project outcomes, lessons learned, outstanding actions, and recommendations for improvement.

## 5. Monitoring and Controlling Across the Lifecycle

Monitoring and controlling is a continuous project management responsibility, not simply a stage that begins after execution.

Throughout the project, the Project Manager or assigned team should:

- Compare actual progress against the approved schedule and milestones.
- Track planned versus actual costs and authorized expenditures.
- Review scope changes and assess their impacts.
- Monitor risks, issues, dependencies, and corrective actions.
- Validate deliverable quality and acceptance.
- Maintain current status reports and performance indicators.
- Communicate material variances and escalate decisions.
- Update project records and forecasts.
- Confirm that changes are approved before implementation when required.

**Typical outputs:** Updated status reports, forecasts, risk and issue registers, change requests, corrective action plans, and approved project updates.

## 6. Applying the Stages to Different Methodologies

| Methodology | How the Stages Are Applied |
|---|---|
| Predictive / Waterfall | Work is generally organized into planned stages with defined approvals and handoffs. Some stages may overlap when approved. |
| Agile | Requirements, design, development, testing, and delivery may repeat across short iterations or sprints. Feedback informs future work. |
| Hybrid | Predictive governance and milestone controls are combined with iterative delivery where appropriate. |

The selected methodology should reflect project complexity, uncertainty, regulatory requirements, stakeholder needs, and delivery constraints.

## 7. Application to the International STEM Education Program

The additional stages can help structure the program's delivery and sustainability activities.

| Stage | Potential Program Application |
|---|---|
| Feasibility and Business Analysis | Validate community needs, target populations, program capacity, funding conditions, and sustainability assumptions. |
| Requirements Gathering | Document student, educator, school, community, technology, and accessibility needs. |
| Design and Solution Development | Develop STEM learning plans, workshop designs, educational resources, and delivery processes. |
| Procurement and Contracting | Evaluate approved vendors for learning materials, technology, equipment, and contracted services. |
| Testing and Validation | Pilot or review learning resources and delivery processes before broader rollout. |
| Implementation and Deployment | Coordinate approved workshops, educational activities, training, and program delivery. |
| Transition and Handover | Establish ongoing responsibilities for program operations, resource maintenance, and partner coordination. |
| Benefits Realization | Track participation, learning outcomes, partner engagement, and sustainability indicators. |

**Funding governance note:** The program has reported $1.5 million in secured local sponsorship funding. Funding availability, restrictions, approved allocations, and expenditures should be verified against sponsorship agreements and financial records before being used in budgets or performance claims.

Maintain the following records as applicable:

- Business case and feasibility assessment.
- Stakeholder register and requirements documentation.
- Requirements traceability matrix.
- Scope statement and work breakdown structure.
- Schedule and milestone plan.
- Budget and financial tracking records.
- Risk, issue, assumption, and dependency registers.
- Procurement and vendor records.
- Quality assurance and test documentation.
- Change request and decision logs.
- Deployment and transition checklists.
- Benefits realization plan and KPI dashboard.
- Lessons-learned register and post-implementation review.

## 9. Key Performance Indicators

Potential indicators include:

- Milestone completion rate.
- Schedule variance.
- Budget variance.
- Deliverable acceptance rate.
- Requirements coverage.
- Defect resolution rate.
- Stakeholder satisfaction.
- Training completion rate.
- Student participation and retention.
- Educational outcome measures.
- Partner engagement.
- Benefits realized against approved targets.

Targets should be defined during planning and adjusted only through the applicable approval process.

## 10. Final Considerations

Additional stages provide more detailed control over project work, but they are not mandatory standalone phases in every organization or methodology. Project Managers should tailor the lifecycle to the project's size, complexity, risks, contractual obligations, and governance requirements.

Monitoring and controlling should continue throughout the lifecycle. Project closure and post-implementation review should confirm deliverable acceptance, outstanding obligations, lessons learned, and any continuing responsibility for benefits tracking.
