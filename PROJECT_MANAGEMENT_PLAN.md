## 1. Project Purpose

The purpose of this project management plan is to establish a structured framework for planning, coordinating, implementing, monitoring, and closing the International STEM Education Program. The plan supports effective resource allocation, stakeholder engagement, financial accountability, risk management, quality assurance, and long-term sustainability.

The program aims to expand access to Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM) education through learning resources, educational workshops, technology, training, and school and community partnerships.

## 2. Define Scope, Deliverables, and Requirements

### Project Scope

The project includes:

- Developing STEM educational activities and learning resources.
- Planning workshops and training programs.
- Identifying technology, equipment, and facility requirements.
- Establishing school and community partnerships.
- Managing project schedules, budgets, and resources.
- Monitoring risks, issues, and project performance.
- Developing sustainability and evaluation plans.

### Key Deliverables

1. Approved Project Charter
2. Requirements Register
3. Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)
4. Project Schedule and Milestone Plan
5. Budget and Cost Management Plan
6. Risk and Issue Register
7. Resource Allocation Plan

13. Sustainability and Project Closure Report

### Requirements Management

Each requirement should have a unique identifier, description, priority, assigned owner, acceptance criteria, and status.

Requirements must be reviewed with appropriate stakeholders and approved before they become part of the project baseline.

## 5. Estimate Costs and Develop the Budget

The reported $1.5 million in local sponsorship funding should be verified against sponsorship agreements, payment records, restrictions, and available balances before being treated as the approved project budget.

### Potential Budget Categories

- STEM equipment and technology
- Educational materials and curriculum resources
- Workshops and training
- Personnel and contracted services
- Facilities and program delivery
- Transportation and logistics
- Marketing and community outreach
- Procurement and vendor services
- Administration and compliance
- Monitoring and evaluation
- Approved contingency reserve

### Budget Management Process

1. Identify project activities and resource requirements.
2. Estimate quantities and unit costs.
3. Document assumptions and funding restrictions.
4. Review estimates with authorized financial representatives.
5. Obtain approval for the budget baseline.
6. Track actual expenses and outstanding commitments.
7. Analyze budget variances.
8. Obtain approval for budget changes.

**Control principle:** Do not report estimated expenses as actual expenditures or assume that all sponsorship funding is unrestricted or immediately available.

## 6. Identify Risks and Mitigation Strategies

| Risk | Potential Impact | Mitigation Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Delayed sponsorship payments | Schedule or delivery delays | Verify payment schedules and monitor cash flow |
| Budget overruns | Reduced resources for planned activities | Use cost estimates, spending controls, and variance reporting |
| Equipment delays | Program readiness issues | Confirm lead times and identify alternatives |
| Insufficient staffing | Delayed or reduced program delivery | Confirm resource availability and backup coverage |
| Partner withdrawal | Reduced participation or access | Document commitments and maintain alternative partners |
| Low participation | Program targets may not be achieved | Improve outreach and review participant feedback |
| Quality concerns | Deliverables may not meet requirements | Establish standards and conduct quality reviews |
| Privacy or safeguarding concerns | Participant harm or compliance issues | Apply appropriate privacy, consent, and safeguarding procedures |
| Unapproved scope changes | Increased cost or schedule pressure | Use formal change control |
| Limited long-term funding | Sustainability challenges | Develop future funding and operating plans |

Each risk should have an owner, likelihood rating, impact rating, response strategy, and review date.

## 9. Develop the Quality Management Plan

Quality management ensures that deliverables meet approved requirements and are appropriate for their intended purpose.

### Quality Activities

- Define acceptance criteria before work begins.
- Review educational materials for accuracy and age appropriateness.
- Confirm equipment meets approved specifications.
- Use readiness checklists before activities.
- Collect appropriate stakeholder feedback.
- Track quality issues and corrective actions.
- Document acceptance of completed deliverables.

### Potential Quality Metrics

- Percentage of deliverables accepted.
- Percentage of planned activities completed.
- Participant and partner feedback.
- Number of unresolved quality issues.
- Time required to close corrective actions.

Targets and measurement methods must be approved before results are reported.

## 10. Develop the Procurement Management Plan

Procurement activities should comply with organizational policies, sponsorship restrictions, contract terms, and applicable laws.

### Procurement Process

1. Define the purchasing requirement.
2. Confirm budget authorization.
3. Establish specifications and acceptance criteria.
4. Obtain quotes or proposals when required.
5. Evaluate vendors using documented criteria.
6. Obtain approval before making commitments.
7. Track orders, contracts, and delivery dates.
8. Inspect and accept goods or services.
9. Reconcile invoices and document procurement closeout.

Vendor selection should consider cost, quality, delivery, service, accessibility, and compliance. Conflicts of interest must be avoided and documented.

## 12. Monitoring, Controlling, and Change Management

Project performance should be compared with approved scope, schedule, budget, quality standards, and KPI baselines.

### Change Control Process

1. Record the proposed change.
2. Document the reason for the change.
3. Assess impacts on scope, cost, schedule, quality, risks, and resources.
4. Submit the request to the authorized decision-maker.
5. Record approval, rejection, or deferral.
6. Update affected project documents.
7. Communicate the decision to relevant stakeholders.
8. Verify implementation.

### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

- Scope: Deliverables completed and accepted.
- Schedule: Milestones achieved on time.
- Cost: Actual spending compared with approved budget.
- Risk: High-priority risks with active response plans.
- Quality: Acceptance results and unresolved issues.
- Participation: Enrollment and attendance against approved targets.
- Engagement: Partner participation and feedback.
- Sustainability: Progress toward future operating and funding plans.

Each KPI should have a definition, owner, data source, reporting frequency, baseline, and approved target.
