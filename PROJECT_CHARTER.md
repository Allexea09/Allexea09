# International STEM Education Program
## Project Charter

### 1. Project Overview

**Project Name:** International STEM Education Program  
**Project Type:** Nonprofit Education and Community Development Initiative  
**Target Population:** Children and young adults ages 5–24 across the United States  
**Reported Secured Funding:** $1,500,000 in local sponsorship funding  
**Planning Horizon:** Three-Year Sustainability Plan  
**Project Management Focus:** Strategic Planning, Budget Oversight, Stakeholder Engagement, Risk Management, Performance Measurement, and Sustainability Planning

---

### 2. Project Purpose and Business Need

The purpose of the International STEM Education Program is to expand access to Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM) education for children and young adults ages 5–24.

The initiative is designed to address educational opportunity gaps by supporting STEM learning resources, educational workshops, technology and equipment, student and teacher training, and school and community partnerships.

The project establishes a structured management framework to support program development, responsible resource allocation, stakeholder collaboration, measurable educational outcomes, and long-term sustainability.

---

### 3. Project Objectives

The project aims to:

1. Expand access to STEM learning opportunities for children and young adults ages 5–24.
2. Develop educational workshops and practical technology-learning opportunities.
3. Support student and teacher training through educational resources and structured learning activities.
4. Establish and strengthen school and community partnerships.
5. Manage the reported $1.5 million in secured local sponsorship funding in accordance with verified funding agreements and approved budgets.
6. Establish key performance indicators (KPIs) to measure program delivery, participation, financial performance, and educational impact.
7. Develop and maintain a three-year sustainability plan.
8. Promote transparent reporting, risk management, and continuous improvement.

---

### 4. Project Scope

#### In Scope

- Program planning and development.
- STEM learning center planning and educational resource coordination.
- Educational workshops and training initiatives.
- Technology and equipment planning.
- Student and teacher training.
- School, nonprofit, and community partnership development.
- Budget planning, expense monitoring, and financial reporting.
- Project milestone and schedule tracking.
- Risk identification and mitigation planning.
- KPI development and performance reporting.
- Stakeholder communication and engagement.
- Three-year sustainability planning and evaluation.

#### Out of Scope

Unless separately approved, the following are excluded from the initial project scope:

- Guaranteed educational or employment outcomes.
- Unapproved capital expenditures or facility construction.
- Activities outside the approved program objectives.
- Expansion beyond available funding, staffing, and operational capacity.
- Financial commitments not supported by authorized agreements or approvals.

### 7. Funding and Budget Governance

The program reports $1.5 million in secured local sponsorship funding.

Financial governance will include:

- Verification and documentation of sponsorship commitments and receipts.
- Development and approval of a detailed program budget.
- Allocation of resources according to program priorities and funding restrictions.
- Periodic comparison of planned budgets against actual expenditures.
- Monitoring of financial variances, outstanding commitments, and available funds.
- Maintenance of appropriate financial records and supporting documentation.
- Regular financial reporting and review.

**Budget Baseline:** To be established using verified funding documentation, approved allocations, operating requirements, and authorized expenditures.

**Planning Assumption:** An even allocation of $500,000 annually over three years is an illustrative scenario only and is not an approved budget unless formally adopted.

### 10. Risks and Mitigation Strategies

| Risk | Potential Impact | Mitigation Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Funding Constraints | Reduced program capacity or delayed delivery | Monitor available funding and align spending with approved priorities |
| Budget Variances | Financial pressure or resource shortfalls | Conduct regular budget reviews and investigate material variances |
| Schedule Delays | Late delivery of project milestones | Track dependencies, assign owners, and maintain contingency plans |
| Limited Participation | Reduced program reach | Coordinate outreach with schools and community partners |
| Resource Shortages | Delays in educational activities | Assess staffing, equipment, and facility requirements in advance |
| Partnership Changes | Disruption to program delivery | Maintain regular communication and document partnership commitments |
| Data Quality Issues | Inaccurate performance reporting | Validate source records and establish consistent reporting procedures |

### 11. Assumptions and Constraints

#### Assumptions

- Reported sponsorship funding will be verified against available records.
- Program activities will be implemented according to approved plans and available resources.
- School and community partners may participate subject to their agreement and capacity.
- Performance metrics will be updated as reliable data becomes available.
- The program will review its sustainability strategy throughout the three-year planning horizon.

#### Constraints

- Applicable nonprofit, financial, educational, and privacy requirements.
- Stakeholder availability and partnership commitments.
- The need to verify reported outcomes before publishing them.

### 12. Project Governance and Change Control

Project governance will establish accountability for scope, schedule, budget, risks, and deliverables.

Proposed governance practices include:

- Regular project progress reviews.
- Documented decisions and action items.
- Review of budget and schedule variances.
- Assessment of risks and corrective actions.
- Formal review of material changes to scope, schedule, or budget.
- Periodic reporting to authorized leadership and relevant stakeholders.

Changes that affect approved funding, program objectives, major deliverables, or timelines should be documented and reviewed by the appropriate decision-makers before implementation.

### 13. Project Success Criteria

Project success will be evaluated against approved objectives and verified evidence of:

1. Delivery of planned STEM education activities.
2. Increased access to educational resources and training opportunities.
3. Active and documented school and community partnerships.
4. Responsible financial management and budget oversight.
5. Progress against approved project milestones.
6. Reliable KPI reporting and documented program results.
7. Completion and review of a practical three-year sustainability plan.

Targets will be defined and approved before being used to evaluate actual performance.
