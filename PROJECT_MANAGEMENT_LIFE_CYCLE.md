# Project Management Life Cycle
## Five-Phase Project Management Framework

## Overview

The Project Management Life Cycle provides a structured framework for managing projects from initial concept through final delivery and closure. It establishes a repeatable, organized approach to defining objectives, managing scope, coordinating resources, controlling costs, mitigating risks, monitoring performance, and delivering measurable business value.

This framework follows five core project management phases: **Initiation, Planning, Execution, Monitoring and Controlling, and Closing.** Each phase includes defined activities, deliverables, stakeholder responsibilities, and performance measures that support transparency, accountability, and successful project outcomes.

---

## 1. Project Initiation

The initiation phase establishes the project's purpose, strategic alignment, business justification, and expected outcomes. Activities include identifying the business problem or opportunity, conducting preliminary feasibility assessments, defining high-level requirements, identifying stakeholders, evaluating constraints, and developing the project charter. The approved charter authorizes the project and establishes its preliminary objectives, scope, assumptions, and success criteria.

### Key Activities

- Identify the business problem, need, or opportunity.
- Define project objectives and expected benefits.
- Conduct preliminary feasibility and business justification assessments.
- Identify stakeholders and decision-makers.
- Establish high-level requirements, assumptions, and constraints.
- Assess initial risks and resource needs.
- Develop and obtain approval for the project charter.

### Key Deliverables

- Business case.
- Feasibility assessment.
- Stakeholder register.
- High-level requirements.
- Approved project charter.

### Success Criteria

The project has a documented business justification, clearly defined objectives, identified stakeholders, and appropriate authorization to proceed.

---

## 2. Project Planning

The planning phase translates project objectives into an actionable management plan. The project manager defines the scope, establishes deliverables, develops the Work Breakdown Structure (WBS), creates the project schedule, identifies milestones and dependencies, estimates costs, allocates resources, and establishes the budget baseline. Additional planning activities address risk management, quality standards, communication protocols, procurement, stakeholder engagement, change control, and project governance.

### Key Activities

- Define project scope and requirements.
- Develop the Work Breakdown Structure (WBS).
- Establish deliverables, milestones, dependencies, and deadlines.
- Estimate project costs and develop the budget baseline.
- Identify required personnel, technology, equipment, and other resources.
- Create the project schedule and resource allocation plan.
- Develop risk assessment and mitigation strategies.
- Establish quality standards and acceptance criteria.
- Develop stakeholder communication and engagement plans.
- Define procurement, change control, and governance procedures.

### Key Deliverables

- Project management plan.
- Scope statement.
- Work Breakdown Structure (WBS).
- Project schedule.
- Approved budget and cost baseline.
- Risk register.
- Communication plan.
- Resource management plan.
- Quality management plan.
- Change control procedures.

### Success Criteria

The project has an actionable and approved plan that aligns scope, schedule, budget, resources, risks, and quality expectations.

---

## 3. Project Execution

The execution phase coordinates people, processes, technology, vendors, and resources to complete the approved project work. The project manager facilitates collaboration, assigns responsibilities, manages stakeholder expectations, coordinates procurement, supports quality assurance, resolves operational challenges, and ensures that deliverables are produced according to established requirements. Progress updates and regular team meetings help maintain alignment with project objectives.

### Key Activities

- Direct and coordinate project work.
- Assign tasks and communicate responsibilities.
- Manage project teams, vendors, and resources.
- Produce approved deliverables and work products.
- Facilitate stakeholder collaboration and team meetings.
- Coordinate procurement and vendor activities.
- Conduct quality assurance activities.
- Resolve operational issues and manage dependencies.
- Communicate project progress and decisions.
- Maintain project documentation and records.

### Key Deliverables

- Completed work packages.
- Project deliverables.
- Project status reports.
- Quality assurance records.
- Meeting minutes.
- Stakeholder communications.
- Updated project documentation.

### Success Criteria

Project work is performed according to the approved plan, deliverables meet established requirements, and stakeholders remain informed and engaged.

---

## 4. Monitoring and Controlling

Monitoring and controlling operates throughout the project to measure actual performance against the approved scope, schedule, budget, and quality baselines. The project manager tracks key performance indicators (KPIs), evaluates variances, monitors risks and issues, manages dependencies, reviews change requests, and implements corrective actions when performance deviates from expectations. This phase supports informed decision-making, effective governance, early issue detection, and controlled project changes.

### Key Activities

- Monitor project scope and prevent unauthorized scope changes.
- Track milestones, dependencies, schedule performance, and deadlines.
- Compare actual expenditures with the approved budget.
- Forecast remaining costs and resource requirements.
- Monitor key performance indicators (KPIs).
- Conduct quality control and verify deliverable acceptance criteria.
- Identify, assess, and manage risks and issues.
- Review and approve change requests through established governance.
- Analyze variances and implement corrective or preventive actions.
- Report project status and performance to stakeholders.
- Maintain accurate records of decisions, changes, and corrective actions.

### Key Deliverables

- KPI dashboards.
- Project performance reports.
- Risk and issue logs.
- Change request records.
- Scope, schedule, and cost variance analyses.
- Updated forecasts.
- Quality control records.
- Corrective action plans.
- Stakeholder status reports.

### Success Criteria

Performance is measured against approved baselines, changes are properly controlled, risks and issues are actively managed, deliverables meet quality requirements, and stakeholders receive accurate and timely status reports.

---

## 5. Project Closing

The closing phase formally concludes the project after deliverables have been completed, reviewed, and accepted by the appropriate stakeholders. Activities include validating requirements, obtaining final acceptance, completing outstanding contractual and financial obligations, transferring deliverables to the operational owner, releasing resources, documenting lessons learned, and archiving project records. A final review evaluates project performance and identifies opportunities for continuous improvement.

### Key Activities

- Verify that approved deliverables are complete.
- Validate requirements and obtain formal stakeholder acceptance.
- Complete outstanding contractual and financial obligations.
- Transfer deliverables, documentation, and operational responsibilities.
- Release project resources and close vendor engagements.
- Conduct a final project performance review.
- Document lessons learned and improvement opportunities.
- Archive project records and supporting documentation.
- Evaluate realized benefits and identify follow-up actions.

### Key Deliverables

- Final project report.
- Acceptance documentation.
- Transition and handover records.
- Lessons-learned register.
- Contract closure documentation.
- Final financial reconciliation.
- Archived project files.
- Benefits evaluation or post-implementation review plan.

### Success Criteria

Project deliverables are formally accepted, outstanding obligations are resolved, operational ownership is transferred, and project records and lessons learned are preserved.

---

## Project Governance and Performance Management

Effective project management integrates governance, communication, risk management, stakeholder engagement, quality assurance, resource coordination, and change management across all five phases. These practices establish clear accountability, maintain alignment with organizational priorities, and help project teams respond to changing requirements and business conditions.

### Core Performance Indicators

| Performance Area | Example Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Scope Management | Approved deliverables completed | Measures delivery against agreed scope |
| Schedule Management | Milestone completion rate and schedule variance | Evaluates progress against the project schedule |
| Cost Management | Budget variance and forecast at completion | Monitors financial performance |
| Quality Management | Deliverable acceptance rate and defect rate | Evaluates deliverable quality |
| Risk Management | Open high-priority risks and mitigation completion | Tracks exposure and response effectiveness |
| Stakeholder Engagement | Stakeholder feedback and satisfaction | Evaluates communication and collaboration |
| Resource Management | Resource utilization and capacity | Assesses resource allocation |
| Business Benefits | Achievement of approved objectives | Evaluates business value and intended outcomes |

Performance targets should be defined in the project management plan and measured using reliable, documented data.

## Methodology and Continuous Improvement

The framework can be adapted to predictive, Agile, or hybrid delivery environments.

### Predictive Project Management

Predictive approaches emphasize detailed upfront planning, defined scope, approved baselines, and sequential execution where requirements are sufficiently stable.

### Agile Project Management

Agile approaches emphasize iterative delivery, prioritized backlogs, frequent stakeholder feedback, and continuous adaptation to changing requirements.

### Hybrid Project Management

Hybrid approaches combine structured governance and baseline controls with iterative execution where flexibility is beneficial.

### Continuous Improvement Activities

- Review project performance and lessons learned.
- Analyze recurring risks, delays, defects, and budget variances.
- Improve estimation, planning, and resource allocation practices.
- Refine stakeholder communication and reporting.
- Incorporate feedback into future project plans.
- Evaluate outcomes and opportunities for process improvement.

## Expected Outcomes

The successful application of this framework supports:

- Clear project objectives, scope, and success criteria.
- Structured schedules, budgets, and resource allocation.
- Improved risk identification and mitigation.
- Transparent reporting and stakeholder accountability.
- Controlled changes and informed decision-making.
- Quality deliverables aligned with approved requirements.
- Formal acceptance, knowledge transfer, and project closure.
- Measurable business value and continuous improvement.

## Application to the International STEM Education Program

This framework can be applied to the International STEM Education Program serving children and young adults ages 5–24 across the United States.

Potential applications include:

- **Initiation:** Establish program objectives, stakeholder requirements, and project authorization.
- **Planning:** Develop the implementation plan, educational resources plan, budget, schedule, risk register, and sustainability strategy.
- **Execution:** Coordinate educational workshops, student and teacher training, technology resources, and community partnerships.
- **Monitoring and Controlling:** Track KPIs, program participation, budget performance, milestones, risks, quality, and stakeholder reporting.
- **Closing:** Evaluate completed activities, document verified outcomes, transition ongoing responsibilities, and capture lessons learned.

The program reports $1.5 million in secured local sponsorship funding and uses a three-year sustainability planning horizon. Financial allocations, implementation milestones, and performance results should be maintained using verified records and approved project documentation.
