# Phase 3: Project Execution and Deliverables
## International STEM Education Program

**Project Management Phase:** Execution  
**Program Duration:** Three-Year Sustainability Planning Horizon  
**Target Audience:** Children and Young Adults Ages 5–24  
**Document Owner:** Project Manager / Program Coordinator  
**Document Status:** Working Draft — Pending Approval

---

## 1. Executive Overview

The Execution phase transforms the approved project management plan into coordinated project activities and tangible deliverables. During this phase, the project manager coordinates teams, manages resources, oversees vendors, communicates progress, monitors quality, resolves issues, and maintains stakeholder engagement.

For the International STEM Education Program, execution is intended to support the delivery of STEM educational resources, workshops, training, technology-related learning activities, and school and community partnerships.

All execution activities should align with the approved scope, schedule, budget, quality requirements, and governance procedures. Deliverables should be documented, reviewed, and accepted by the appropriate authority.

**Important:** This document defines the execution approach. Individual activities should be marked complete only when supporting evidence confirms completion.

---

## 2. Execution Objectives

The objectives of the execution phase are to:

- Perform work according to the approved project management plan.
- Coordinate project teams, resources, and work packages.
- Manage vendors, contractors, and partner commitments.
- Conduct project meetings and communicate progress.
- Maintain quality standards and deliverable acceptance criteria.
- Identify and resolve issues that may delay project delivery.
- Maintain stakeholder engagement and accountability.
- Produce, validate, and deliver approved project outputs.
- Document decisions, changes, and lessons learned.

---

## 3. Coordinate Project Teams and Resources

Effective resource coordination ensures that assigned work is completed by appropriately qualified people with the necessary tools, information, and support.

### Key Activities

- Confirm project roles and responsibilities.
- Assign work packages to responsible team members.
- Communicate task requirements, deadlines, and dependencies.
- Verify resource availability and capacity.
- Coordinate educators, trainers, volunteers, and delivery partners.
- Monitor workload and resource utilization.
- Address staffing gaps and competing priorities.
- Escalate resource constraints when additional authorization is required.

### Resource Coordination Deliverables

- Resource assignment register.
- Responsibility matrix.
- Work assignment tracker.
- Resource availability report.
- Team meeting action log.

### Performance Measures

- Percentage of work packages with assigned owners.
- Percentage of assigned tasks completed by agreed deadlines.
- Number of unresolved resource constraints.
- Resource utilization against approved capacity.

---

## 4. Manage Vendors and Contractors

Vendor and contractor management ensures that procured goods and services meet approved specifications, contractual requirements, delivery schedules, and quality standards.

### Key Activities

1. Review approved purchase orders and contracts.
2. Confirm vendor responsibilities and deliverables.
3. Communicate specifications and delivery expectations.
4. Track procurement and delivery milestones.
5. Monitor vendor performance and service quality.
6. Document delays, defects, and contract issues.
7. Verify goods and services before acceptance.
8. Coordinate invoice review and payment authorization.
9. Escalate contractual concerns through authorized channels.

### Vendor Performance Measures

- Percentage of deliveries received on schedule.
- Percentage of deliverables meeting specifications.
- Number of unresolved vendor issues.
- Procurement cost variance against approved estimates.
- Vendor responsiveness and service performance.

All purchasing and contractual commitments must follow applicable organizational policies and approval limits.

---

## 5. Conduct Meetings and Communicate Progress

Regular communication helps the project team coordinate activities, address obstacles, maintain accountability, and keep stakeholders informed.

### Meeting Framework

| Meeting Type | Purpose | Suggested Frequency | Participants |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project Team Meeting | Review tasks, dependencies, and immediate issues | Weekly or as needed | Project Manager and delivery team |
| Project Status Review | Evaluate schedule, budget, risks, and deliverables | Monthly | Project Manager and project leads |
| Sponsor Review | Address decisions, escalations, and strategic concerns | Monthly or as agreed | Sponsor and Project Manager |
| Vendor Review | Review procurement, delivery, and performance | Per contract or as needed | Procurement Lead and vendor |
| Stakeholder Update | Share approved progress and collect feedback | As agreed | Relevant stakeholders |
| Quality Review | Validate deliverables and resolve defects | At key delivery points | Quality Lead and deliverable owners |

### Communication Deliverables

- Meeting agendas.
- Meeting minutes.
- Decision logs.
- Action-item registers.
- Project status reports.
- Stakeholder communication records.

Every action item should have an assigned owner, due date, and status.

## 6. Execute Tasks According to the Project Plan

Execution activities should follow the approved Work Breakdown Structure (WBS), schedule, budget, and documented requirements.

### Execution Process

1. Review the approved work package and acceptance criteria.
2. Confirm resources, dependencies, and prerequisites.
3. Assign work to the responsible individual or team.
4. Perform the scheduled activity.
5. Record progress and supporting evidence.
6. Verify the output against requirements.
7. Submit the deliverable for review or acceptance.
8. Update project records and report completion status.

### STEM Program Examples

Planned execution activities may include:

- Preparing STEM workshop materials.
- Coordinating educational sessions and training.
- Arranging approved technology and equipment.
- Working with schools and community organizations.
- Coordinating instructors and program volunteers.
- Maintaining appropriate attendance and delivery records.
- Collecting feedback to support program evaluation.

These examples are proposed activities and should be adapted to the approved program scope.

## 7. Manage Quality Assurance

Quality assurance focuses on the processes used to prevent defects and improve the consistency of project delivery. Quality control verifies whether individual deliverables meet their acceptance criteria.

### Quality Assurance Activities

- Follow approved project procedures.
- Review work products against requirements.
- Confirm team members understand quality expectations.
- Conduct peer reviews and readiness checks.
- Verify that educational materials are appropriate for the intended age groups.
- Confirm equipment meets approved specifications.
- Document defects and corrective actions.
- Obtain acceptance from the designated approver.

### Quality Performance Indicators

| Indicator | Measurement |
|---|---|
| Deliverable Acceptance Rate | Accepted deliverables divided by deliverables submitted |
| First-Pass Acceptance | Deliverables accepted without rework |
| Defect Rate | Identified defects relative to items reviewed |
| Corrective Action Closure | Corrective actions completed by their due dates |
| Readiness Compliance | Required readiness checks completed before delivery |

Quality targets should be established and approved before performance is measured.

## 8. Resolve Issues and Remove Roadblocks

Issue management helps prevent operational problems from becoming major schedule, cost, quality, or stakeholder concerns.

### Issue Resolution Process

1. Identify and document the issue.
2. Assess its impact and urgency.
3. Assign an issue owner.
4. Determine the root cause.
5. Develop resolution options.
6. Implement an authorized corrective action.
7. Monitor progress toward resolution.
8. Verify the outcome.
9. Close the issue and record lessons learned.

### Common Execution Issues

- Resource or staffing shortages.
- Delayed equipment or materials.
- Unclear requirements.
- Missed task deadlines.
- Vendor performance problems.
- Conflicting stakeholder expectations.
- Quality defects.
- Budget constraints.

### Escalation

Issues should be escalated when they exceed the project manager's authority, threaten a major milestone, affect approved funding, create significant compliance concerns, or place project objectives at risk.

## 9. Manage Stakeholder Engagement

Stakeholder engagement maintains alignment between project activities and the needs of sponsors, educators, community partners, vendors, and intended participants.

### Key Activities

- Communicate relevant project updates.
- Confirm stakeholder expectations.
- Track partnership commitments.
- Obtain feedback on activities and deliverables.
- Address concerns promptly.
- Document decisions and agreed actions.
- Maintain appropriate privacy and safeguarding practices.
- Escalate unresolved stakeholder conflicts.

### Stakeholder Engagement Measures

- Participation in scheduled project reviews.
- Completion of agreed stakeholder actions.
- Timeliness of responses to concerns.
- Feedback collected through approved methods.
- Number of unresolved stakeholder issues.

Stakeholder communications should be accurate, accessible, and consistent with approved disclosure and privacy requirements.

## 10. Produce and Deliver Project Outputs

Project outputs are the products, services, documents, or results created by performing approved project work.

### Potential STEM Program Outputs

- Educational resources and workshop materials.
- Training sessions and learning activities.
- Technology and equipment prepared for approved use.
- School and community partnership documentation.
- Program delivery records.
- Quality review and acceptance records.
- Performance dashboards and status reports.
- Sustainability planning documentation.

### Deliverable Acceptance Process

1. Confirm the output matches the approved scope.
2. Review the deliverable against documented requirements.
3. Complete quality checks.
4. Resolve identified defects.
5. Submit the deliverable to the authorized approver.
6. Record acceptance or required revisions.
7. Store supporting documentation.
8. Update the deliverable register.

Deliverables should not be classified as complete until acceptance requirements have been met and completion has been documented.

The project manager should track actual work performed against the approved project plan.

### Execution Dashboard Metrics

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Tasks Completed | Measures progress against planned work |
| Deliverables Accepted | Tracks completed and approved outputs |

## 12. Change Management During Execution

Execution may reveal new requirements, resource constraints, or opportunities for improvement. Proposed changes must be evaluated before implementation.

### Change Control Steps

1. Document the requested change and justification.
2. Assess impacts on scope, schedule, cost, quality, risks, and resources.
3. Identify alternatives.
4. Submit the request to the authorized decision-maker.
5. Record approval, rejection, or deferral.
6. Update the project plan and affected documents.
7. Communicate the decision.
8. Verify implementation.

The project team should not make unauthorized changes to approved baselines or contractual commitments.

## 14. Execution Phase Success Criteria

The execution phase supports success when:

- Approved project activities are performed.
- Work packages have clear owners.
- Resources are coordinated effectively.
- Vendors and contractors meet approved requirements.
- Project progress is communicated consistently.
- Deliverables meet documented quality standards.
- Issues and roadblocks are tracked and resolved.
- Stakeholders remain appropriately engaged.
- Outputs are reviewed and accepted.
- Project records provide evidence of work performed and results achieved.

Final execution performance should be evaluated against the approved project management plan and formally authorized targets.
