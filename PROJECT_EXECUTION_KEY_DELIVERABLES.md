## 2. Purpose

This document establishes a structured approach for identifying, producing, reviewing, tracking, and accepting the key deliverables generated during project execution.

The deliverables provide evidence of completed work, project progress, quality control, decisions, and communication with stakeholders. Each deliverable should be maintained in the project repository or designated document management system and reviewed according to the approved project management plan.

**Status definitions:**
- **Not Started:** Work has not begun.
- **In Progress:** Work is underway.
- **Under Review:** Deliverable is awaiting review or approval.
- **Accepted:** Authorized reviewer has confirmed acceptance.
- **On Hold:** Work is temporarily paused.
- **At Risk:** A concern may affect delivery, quality, cost, or schedule.

Use these statuses to report actual progress; do not mark a deliverable as complete or accepted without supporting evidence.

## 4. Deliverable Requirements

### 4.1 Completed Work Packages

Each completed work package should include:

- Work package name and unique identification number.
- Description of planned activities and scope.
- Assigned owner and participating team members.
- Completion date and supporting evidence.
- Variances from the approved plan.
- Outstanding issues or dependencies.
- Review and acceptance confirmation.

**Completion standard:** All required activities are completed, documented, and reviewed against the approved work package requirements.

### 4.2 Project Deliverables

Project deliverables may include:

- Age-appropriate STEM curriculum and instructional materials.
- Student learning activities, workshops, and educational events.
- Teacher, mentor, or facilitator training resources.
- Technology and equipment requirements.
- School and community partnership materials.
- Program implementation and sustainability documentation.
- Student participation and learning assessment tools.

**Acceptance standard:** Each deliverable has documented requirements, an assigned owner, a review process, and evidence of acceptance by the authorized stakeholder.

### 4.3 Status Reports

Each reporting period should capture:

- Reporting period and report preparation date.
- Overall project status.
- Completed activities and verified accomplishments.
- Planned versus actual milestone dates.
- Budget performance and approved expenditures.
- Key performance indicators (KPIs).
- Risks, issues, dependencies, and decisions needed.
- Corrective actions, responsible owners, and due dates.
- Activities planned for the next reporting period.

**Acceptance standard:** Information is current, supported by project records, and communicated to the appropriate stakeholders.

### 4.4 Quality Assurance Records

Quality records should document:

- Applicable requirements and quality standards.
- Review and inspection checklists.
- Testing or evaluation results, where applicable.
- Defects, gaps, and nonconformities.
- Corrective and preventive actions.
- Assigned action owners and resolution dates.
- Retesting or verification results.
- Final quality review and acceptance.

**Acceptance standard:** Required reviews are completed, findings are addressed or formally documented, and approval evidence is retained.

### 4.5 Meeting Minutes

Meeting minutes should include:

- Meeting title, date, time, and participants.
- Meeting objectives and agenda.
- Key discussion points.
- Decisions and approvals.
- Action items and assigned owners.
- Target completion dates.
- Unresolved questions and escalations.
- Next meeting date, when applicable.

**Acceptance standard:** Minutes accurately capture decisions and actions and are shared with the appropriate participants.

### 4.6 Stakeholder Communications

Communication records may include:

- Sponsor and funding partner updates.
- School and community partner correspondence.
- Project team announcements.
- Milestone and event notifications.
- Progress reports and presentations.
- Escalation notices and change notifications.
- Stakeholder feedback and responses.

**Acceptance standard:** Communications are accurate, timely, appropriate for the audience, and retained in accordance with project policies.

## 5. Deliverable Review and Acceptance Workflow

1. **Produce:** The assigned owner develops the deliverable according to approved requirements.
2. **Document:** Supporting files, completion evidence, and relevant project records are collected.
3. **Review:** The designated reviewer checks scope, accuracy, quality, and completeness.
4. **Resolve:** Identified gaps are corrected or formally recorded with an action plan.
5. **Approve:** The authorized stakeholder accepts the deliverable or returns it for revision.
6. **Record:** The deliverable register is updated with status, dates, reviewer, and evidence location.
7. **Archive:** The approved version is stored in the designated repository with appropriate access controls.

## 7. Document Control and Record Management

- Maintain a single authoritative version of each approved deliverable.
- Use consistent file names, version numbers, and dates.
- Restrict access to financial, personal, student, and confidential sponsor information.
- Obtain required approvals before publishing external communications.
- Retain records according to applicable organizational policies and legal requirements.
- Link deliverables to the relevant project milestone, work package, or requirement.
- Record changes to approved deliverables through the project change control process.

## 8. Execution Phase Success Criteria

The execution phase is considered adequately documented when:

- Required work packages have completion evidence.
- Project deliverables have been reviewed against approved requirements.
- Status reports reflect verified project performance.
- Quality assurance records identify findings and resolutions.
- Meeting minutes capture decisions and action items.
- Stakeholder communications are recorded and traceable.
- Outstanding issues and acceptance decisions are documented.
- Project records are organized for monitoring, controlling, and eventual closure.

## 9. Reporting and Governance

The Project Manager is responsible for coordinating deliverable tracking and reporting. Work package owners are responsible for producing completion evidence. Reviewers and authorized stakeholders are responsible for quality review and acceptance within their assigned authority.

Any material deviation affecting scope, schedule, budget, quality, or approved requirements should be escalated and handled under the project's change control and governance procedures.
