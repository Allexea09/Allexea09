## 1. Executive Overview

Project governance and performance management establish the leadership structure, decision-making authority, accountability standards, oversight procedures, and measurement systems required to manage a project effectively.

This framework is designed to support the International STEM Education Program by aligning project activities with strategic objectives, managing financial and operational risks, measuring progress, strengthening stakeholder communication, and promoting long-term sustainability.

The governance model defines who makes decisions, who is responsible for deliverables, how performance is evaluated, and how project issues and changes are escalated.

The performance management framework establishes measurable indicators to monitor scope, schedule, cost, quality, participation, stakeholder engagement, and sustainability.

The reported $1.5 million in local sponsorship funding should be reconciled with supporting agreements, payment records, restrictions, and available balances before being used as the approved financial baseline.

## 2. Governance Objectives

The governance framework is intended to:

- Establish clear roles, responsibilities, and decision-making authority.
- Align project activities with approved program objectives.
- Maintain accountability for deliverables, resources, and expenditures.
- Establish approval procedures for scope, schedule, and budget changes.
- Monitor risks, issues, dependencies, and corrective actions.
- Ensure reliable performance reporting and documentation.
- Promote ethical conduct, transparency, and responsible financial management.
- Support continuous improvement and long-term program sustainability.

## 3. Project Governance Structure

The proposed governance structure includes the following roles.

### Project Sponsor

The Project Sponsor provides strategic direction and authorizes major project decisions.

Responsibilities:
- Approve the project charter and major deliverables.
- Confirm alignment with program objectives.
- Review significant budget, schedule, and scope changes.
- Resolve escalated issues beyond the project manager's authority.
- Review performance reports and sustainability risks.

### Project Manager

The Project Manager coordinates the overall project life cycle and maintains alignment between approved objectives, scope, schedule, budget, resources, and deliverables.

Responsibilities:
- Develop and maintain project management documentation.
- Coordinate work packages and project resources.
- Monitor schedule, cost, scope, and quality.
- Maintain risk, issue, and change registers.
- Prepare project status reports.
- Escalate significant variances and decisions.
- Track corrective actions and document lessons learned.

### Program or Education Lead

Responsibilities:
- Define educational requirements and program activities.
- Coordinate instructors, learning resources, and training.
- Confirm readiness for planned educational activities.
- Monitor delivery quality and collect appropriate feedback.
- Report implementation issues and improvement opportunities.

### Finance Lead

Responsibilities:
- Validate funding commitments and restrictions.
- Maintain approved budget and expenditure records.
- Monitor commitments, actual costs, and forecasts.
- Investigate financial variances.
- Support financial reporting and reconciliation.
- Confirm that expenditures follow applicable authorization requirements.

### Quality and Evaluation Lead

Responsibilities:
- Define quality standards and acceptance criteria.
- Establish KPI definitions and data collection methods.
- Validate performance information.
- Coordinate evaluation activities.
- Monitor corrective actions and lessons learned.

### Stakeholder and Communications Lead

Responsibilities:
- Maintain stakeholder information and communication schedules.
- Coordinate project updates and feedback.
- Document stakeholder commitments and concerns.
- Track communication actions and unresolved matters.

**Governance principle:** Roles should be assigned to named individuals only after their responsibilities, authority, and availability have been confirmed.

## 4. Decision-Making and Approval Authority

Project decisions should be made at the appropriate level and documented.

| Decision Type | Recommended Authority | Required Documentation |
|---|---|---|
| Project objectives and charter | Project Sponsor | Approved charter |
| Routine task coordination | Project Manager | Task tracker or meeting notes |
| Acceptance of assigned deliverables | Designated deliverable owner or approver | Acceptance record |
| Budget approval | Authorized financial approver and sponsor, as applicable | Approved budget |
| Material scope changes | Project Sponsor or designated change authority | Change request and decision |
| Schedule changes affecting major milestones | Project Manager within delegated authority; sponsor for material changes | Updated schedule and approval |
| Procurement commitments | Authorized procurement or financial approver | Purchase approval and supporting records |
| Significant risk escalation | Project Sponsor or designated governance body | Risk record and decision |
| Project closure | Project Sponsor or authorized approver | Closure approval |

Specific financial thresholds and approval limits must be established by the responsible organization. This document does not independently authorize spending or contractual commitments.

## 5. Governance Meetings and Reporting Cadence

| Meeting or Report | Purpose | Suggested Frequency | Participants |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project Team Meeting | Review tasks, dependencies, and immediate issues | Weekly or as needed | Project Manager and delivery team |
| Project Status Review | Review scope, schedule, budget, risks, and KPIs | Monthly | Project Manager and relevant leads |
| Sponsor Review | Resolve major decisions and review strategic alignment | Monthly or quarterly, as agreed | Sponsor and Project Manager |
| Financial Review | Review expenditure, commitments, forecasts, and restrictions | Monthly or per funding requirements | Finance Lead and authorized leadership |
| Risk Review | Evaluate high-priority risks and response effectiveness | Monthly or when risk changes | Project Manager and risk owners |
| Sustainability Review | Review future operating needs and funding plans | Quarterly or at approved milestones | Sponsor and program leadership |
| Project Closure Review | Confirm deliverables, financial reconciliation, and lessons learned | At closure | Sponsor and project team |

Meeting frequency should be adjusted to project complexity, risk level, delivery pace, and stakeholder requirements.

## 6. Performance Management Framework

Performance management compares actual results with approved targets and baselines. It helps the project team identify variances, evaluate corrective actions, and make informed decisions.

Performance reporting should distinguish among:

- **Baseline:** The formally approved plan or target.
- **Actual:** Verified results recorded to date.
- **Variance:** The difference between actual performance and the approved baseline.
- **Forecast:** The current estimate of future performance.
- **Corrective action:** An approved action intended to address an identified variance.
- **Status:** The current condition of a deliverable, milestone, risk, or KPI.

Performance targets must be established and approved before results are assessed against them.

## 7. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

The following KPI framework is proposed for project oversight.

| KPI Category | Measurement | Data Source | Reporting Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope Performance | Percentage of planned deliverables accepted | Deliverable register | Monthly |
| Schedule Performance | Percentage of milestones achieved on time | Baseline schedule | Monthly |
| Budget Performance | Actual costs and commitments compared with approved budget | Financial records | Monthly |
| Risk Management | Number of high-priority risks without an adequate response | Risk register | Monthly |
| Issue Resolution | Percentage of issues resolved by agreed due dates | Issue register | Monthly |
| Quality Performance | Deliverables accepted and unresolved quality issues | Quality records | Monthly |
| Program Participation | Enrollment or attendance compared with approved targets | Verified participation records | Per activity and reporting period |
| Stakeholder Engagement | Participation, feedback, and outstanding concerns | Engagement records | Quarterly or as agreed |
| Procurement Performance | Deliveries received on time and meeting specifications | Purchase and acceptance records | Monthly |
| Sustainability Progress | Completion of approved sustainability milestones | Sustainability plan | Quarterly |

Each KPI should have a named owner, calculation method, data source, reporting period, baseline, target, and validation procedure.

## 8. Performance Status Definitions

Use consistent status definitions in reports.

- **On Track:** Performance is within approved tolerances and no material intervention is currently required.
- **At Risk:** A potential variance or unresolved risk may affect a target or milestone.
- **Off Track:** An approved target or tolerance has been exceeded or is forecast to be missed.
- **Complete:** The deliverable has met its acceptance criteria and completion has been documented.
- **Not Started:** Work has not begun.
- **Pending Approval:** A decision or acceptance is required before proceeding.

Status ratings must be based on documented evidence rather than assumptions.

## 9. Financial Governance and Budget Oversight

Financial governance supports responsible use of sponsorship funding and other authorized resources.

### Financial Controls

1. Verify sponsorship agreements and funding conditions.
2. Establish an approved budget and cost baseline.
3. Assign authorized approvers for financial commitments.
4. Track actual expenditures and outstanding commitments.
5. Compare costs against the approved budget.
6. Forecast remaining costs and funding needs.
7. Investigate material variances.
8. Document approvals for budget changes.
9. Reconcile financial records at project milestones and closure.

### Financial Performance Measures

- Approved budget.
- Actual expenditures to date.
- Outstanding financial commitments.
- Forecast cost to complete.
- Forecast total project cost.
- Budget variance.
- Funding received and remaining available funding, where verified.

Funding received, sponsorship commitments, budget authorization, and actual expenditures are separate measures and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## 10. Risk Governance and Escalation

Risk governance provides a consistent process for identifying, evaluating, responding to, and escalating uncertainties.

### Risk Management Process

1. Identify the risk and its potential cause.
2. Evaluate likelihood and impact.
3. Assign a risk owner.
4. Select an appropriate response.
5. Define response actions and due dates.
6. Monitor changes in exposure.
7. Escalate risks exceeding approved thresholds.
8. Document outcomes and lessons learned.

### Potential Strategic Risks

- Delayed or restricted sponsorship funding.
- Budget overruns or unexpected operating costs.
- Delays in procurement or resource availability.
- Insufficient staffing or partner participation.
- Low participation relative to approved targets.
- Privacy, safeguarding, accessibility, or compliance concerns.
- Unapproved changes to project scope.
- Inadequate resources for long-term sustainability.

The project sponsor should review significant risks that may affect approved objectives, funding, legal obligations, or project viability.

## 11. Change Control and Configuration Management

Changes to approved project documents and baselines must be controlled.

### Change Control Procedure

1. Submit and document the proposed change.
2. Identify the business or program justification.
3. Assess the impact on scope, schedule, budget, quality, resources, risks, and stakeholders.
4. Identify alternatives and recommend an approach.
5. Obtain approval from the authorized decision-maker.
6. Record the decision and rationale.
7. Update affected project documents.
8. Communicate approved changes.
9. Verify implementation.

Maintain version control for the project charter, management plan, schedule, budget, WBS, risk register, KPI definitions, and other controlled documents.

## 12. Data Quality and Reporting Integrity

Reliable performance management depends on accurate, timely, consistent, and traceable data.

### Data Governance Requirements

- Define the source and owner of each KPI.
- Use consistent calculation methods and reporting periods.
- Verify data before publication.
- Retain supporting records according to applicable requirements.
- Restrict access to sensitive or personally identifiable information.
- Collect only information needed for legitimate program purposes.
- Apply appropriate consent, privacy, safeguarding, and access controls.
- Correct reporting errors and document material adjustments.

Participant information, especially information about minors, should be handled using appropriate privacy and safeguarding procedures.

## 13. Stakeholder Accountability and Transparency

Stakeholders should receive information appropriate to their roles and decision-making needs.

### Reporting Principles

- Reports should be accurate and evidence-based.
- Material risks and variances should be disclosed promptly.
- Financial information should be reconciled to supporting records.
- Decisions and approvals should be documented.
- Stakeholder feedback should be recorded and addressed.
- Planned activities should be clearly distinguished from completed work.
- Reported outcomes should be supported by verified evidence.

Sponsor reporting should follow the relevant funding agreements and authorized disclosure requirements.

## 14. Corrective Action and Continuous Improvement

When performance falls outside approved tolerances, the project team should determine the cause and develop an appropriate response.

### Corrective Action Process

1. Identify the variance or performance problem.
2. Analyze its root cause.
3. Determine the impact on project objectives.
4. Assign a corrective-action owner.
5. Establish an action plan and due date.
6. Obtain approval when required.
7. Track implementation.
8. Verify whether the action resolved the issue.
9. Document lessons learned.

Preventive actions should also be considered when trends indicate that a future variance is likely.

## 15. Three-Year Sustainability Governance

The program's three-year sustainability planning horizon should include recurring reviews of financial viability, program delivery capacity, partnerships, educational resources, and future operating responsibilities.

### Sustainability Oversight Areas

- Funding outlook and financial commitments.
- Estimated operating costs and resource requirements.
- Sponsor and community partnership continuity.
- Availability of educators, volunteers, and delivery partners.
- Technology and equipment maintenance.
- Program participation and educational feedback.
- Ownership of continuing operations.
- Future funding and resource-development opportunities.

Sustainability milestones and targets should be formally approved and reviewed using documented evidence. Future funding should not be presented as secured until supporting commitments are verified.

## 16. Governance Deliverables

The governance and performance management process should maintain the following records:

- Approved Project Charter.
- Project Management Plan.
- Work Breakdown Structure.
- Baseline Schedule and Milestone Register.
- Approved Budget and Financial Reports.
- Requirements and Deliverable Registers.
- Risk and Issue Registers.
- Change Request Log.
- Stakeholder Register.
- Communication Plan and Meeting Records.
- Quality Review and Acceptance Records.
- KPI Dashboard and Status Reports.
- Sustainability Review.
- Lessons-Learned Register.
- Final Project Closure Report.

## 17. Project Success Criteria

Project governance and performance management will be considered effective when:

- Decision-making authority is documented.
- Project roles and accountability are assigned.
- Scope, schedule, and budget baselines are approved.
- Financial commitments and expenditures are traceable.
- Risks and issues are reviewed and addressed.
- Performance indicators use consistent, verified data.
- Changes follow documented approval procedures.
- Stakeholders receive timely and accurate reports.
- Deliverables meet approved acceptance criteria.
- Sustainability and closure decisions are documented.

Final project success must be evaluated against formally approved objectives, targets, and acceptance criteria.
