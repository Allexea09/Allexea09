# Develop the Project Management Plan
## International STEM Education Program

**Project Phase:** Planning  
**Project Management Process:** Develop Project Management Plan  
**Program Duration:** Three-Year Sustainability Planning Horizon  
**Target Audience:** Children and Young Adults Ages 5–24  
**Reported Funding:** $1.5 Million in Local Sponsorship Funding  
**Document Status:** Working Draft — Pending Approval  
**Document Owner:** Project Manager / Program Coordinator

---

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to establish an integrated project management plan that defines how the International STEM Education Program will be organized, planned, executed, monitored, controlled, and closed.

The plan combines scope, schedule, cost, resources, risk, quality, procurement, communications, and stakeholder management into a coordinated framework. It provides a structured approach for aligning project activities with approved objectives, managing constraints, tracking performance, and supporting long-term sustainability.

The reported $1.5 million in local sponsorship funding must be validated against supporting agreements, payment records, restrictions, and available balances before it is used as an approved project budget.

---

## 2. Define Scope, Deliverables, and Requirements

### 2.1 Project Scope

The proposed project scope includes:

- Planning STEM educational activities and workshops.
- Identifying educational resources, technology, and equipment.
- Coordinating student and educator training.
- Establishing school and community partnerships.
- Planning staffing, facilities, and operational resources.
- Developing schedules, budgets, and performance measures.
- Managing risks, issues, changes, and dependencies.
- Establishing sustainability and project evaluation processes.

Activities outside the approved objectives or budget require review and authorization before implementation.

### 2.2 Key Deliverables

| Deliverable | Description | Acceptance Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Project Charter | Defines objectives, scope, sponsor, and authority | Formal approval |
| Requirements Register | Records stakeholder needs and expectations | Requirements reviewed and approved |
| Work Breakdown Structure | Organizes project scope into work packages | Scope coverage reviewed |
| Project Schedule | Defines tasks, durations, milestones, and dependencies | Schedule baseline approved |
| Budget Plan | Defines cost estimates, funding, and financial controls | Authorized budget approval |
| Risk Register | Identifies risks and response strategies | Risk owners and responses assigned |
| Resource Plan | Identifies roles, skills, and resource needs | Responsibilities confirmed |
| Communication Plan | Defines reporting and communication methods | Stakeholder needs addressed |
| Quality Plan | Establishes quality standards and acceptance procedures | Quality criteria approved |
| Procurement Plan | Defines purchasing and vendor controls | Procurement approach approved |
| Stakeholder Plan | Defines engagement and relationship management | Stakeholder responsibilities documented |
| Performance Dashboard | Tracks KPIs, milestones, costs, and risks | Data sources and reporting methods defined |
| Closure Report | Documents results, acceptance, and lessons learned | Authorized closure approval |

### 2.3 Requirements Management

Requirements should be collected from project sponsors, educators, program leadership, funding stakeholders, community partners, and other relevant participants.

Each requirement should include:

- Unique requirement identification number.
- Description and business justification.
- Source and stakeholder.
- Priority and assigned owner.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Status and approval history.

Requirements must be traceable to project deliverables and approved objectives.

The project schedule should be developed after scope, activity estimates, resource availability, and the project start date are confirmed.

### 4.1 Proposed Milestone Plan

| Milestone | Dependency | Completion Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Charter Approved | Sponsor review | Approved charter |
| Requirements Finalized | Stakeholder consultation | Approved requirements register |
| WBS Completed | Scope definition | Reviewed WBS |

### 4.2 Scheduling Activities

- Identify tasks and their required durations.
- Determine task dependencies and sequencing.
- Assign task owners and resource requirements.

- Account for procurement lead times and approval periods.
- Establish baseline dates after formal approval.
- Track actual completion dates against the baseline.
- Document schedule changes and corrective actions.

Actual dates should be entered only after the project calendar and authorized start date are confirmed.

## 5. Estimate Costs and Develop the Budget

### 5.1 Budget Planning

The budget should reflect approved project scope, resource requirements, delivery methods, and verified funding availability.

Potential cost categories include:

- STEM equipment and technology.
- Educational materials and curriculum resources.
- Workshops, training, and program delivery.
- Personnel and contracted services.
- Facilities and operational expenses.
- Transportation and logistics.
- Procurement and vendor services.
- Marketing and community outreach.
- Administration and compliance.
- Monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.
- Approved contingency reserves.

### 5.2 Budget Development Process

1. Identify the resources required for each work package.
2. Estimate quantities, unit costs, and timing.
3. Document cost assumptions and exclusions.
4. Verify funding commitments and restrictions.
5. Review estimates with authorized financial representatives.
6. Obtain approval for the budget baseline.
7. Track actual costs and outstanding commitments.
8. Compare actual and forecast costs against the approved budget.
9. Investigate variances and implement approved corrective actions.

### 5.3 Financial Controls

Financial monitoring should distinguish between:

- Sponsorship commitments.
- Funding received.
- Approved budget allocations.
- Actual expenditures.
- Outstanding commitments.
- Forecast costs to complete.
- Remaining available funds.

The reported $1.5 million in sponsorship funding should not automatically be treated as unrestricted or fully available cash.

## 6. Identify Risks and Mitigation Strategies

| Risk | Potential Impact | Mitigation Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Delayed sponsorship funding | Activities may be delayed | Verify payment schedules and monitor cash flow |
| Budget overruns | Reduced program resources | Establish cost controls and conduct variance reviews |
| Equipment delivery delays | Schedule disruption | Confirm lead times and identify alternatives |
| Insufficient staffing | Reduced delivery capacity | Confirm resource availability and backup coverage |
| Partner withdrawal | Reduced participation or access | Document commitments and develop alternate partnerships |
| Low participant engagement | Program targets may be missed | Improve outreach and evaluate feedback |
| Quality concerns | Deliverables may not meet requirements | Establish quality criteria and conduct reviews |
| Privacy or safeguarding issues | Compliance and participant safety concerns | Apply appropriate safeguards, consent, and access controls |
| Unapproved scope changes | Cost and schedule increases | Use formal change control |
| Limited future funding | Sustainability challenges | Develop future operating and funding plans |

Each risk should have an owner, likelihood rating, impact rating, mitigation actions, and review date.

## 9. Develop the Quality Management Plan

Quality management ensures that project deliverables meet approved requirements and are suitable for their intended purpose.

### Quality Activities

- Define acceptance criteria before work begins.
- Review educational resources for accuracy and age appropriateness.
- Verify equipment against approved specifications.
- Use readiness checklists before workshops and training.
- Collect stakeholder feedback using appropriate methods.
- Document defects, issues, and corrective actions.
- Obtain formal acceptance for completed deliverables.

### Quality Performance Measures

- Percentage of deliverables accepted.
- Percentage of activities completed as planned.
- Participant and partner feedback.
- Number of unresolved quality issues.
- Timeliness of corrective-action closure.

Targets and measurement methods should be approved before performance is reported.

---

## 10. Develop the Procurement Management Plan

Procurement must follow applicable organizational policies, sponsorship restrictions, contract requirements, and laws.

### Procurement Process

1. Define purchasing needs and specifications.
2. Confirm budget authorization.
3. Determine whether competitive quotes or additional approvals are required.
4. Evaluate vendors using documented criteria.
5. Obtain authorization before making commitments.
6. Track orders, contracts, warranties, and delivery dates.
7. Inspect and accept goods or services.
8. Verify invoices and document payments.
9. Complete contract and procurement closeout.

Vendor selection criteria may include cost, quality, reliability, delivery, accessibility, technical support, and compliance.

Conflicts of interest should be avoided and documented.
